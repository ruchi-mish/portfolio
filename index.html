Risk and fraudTry it yourself

Where would you draw the fraud line?

Every payment gets a risk score from 0 (looks normal) to 1 (looks suspicious). A fraud model has to pick a cut-off. This is a toy version, so you can feel the trade-off I practice on real risk problems.

Ordinary paymentsFraudGood payments blockedFraud caught
0.50

Illustrative numbers, not real data: two bell curves with 2% of payments fraudulent, drawn at the same height so both stay visible. Where to put the line depends on what a missed fraud costs compared with an annoyed customer.

  1. Read the curves. Grey is ordinary payments, mostly scoring low. Red is fraud, scoring higher. They overlap, so no line is perfect.
  2. Move the line. Every payment scoring above it gets blocked. Drag the slider.
  3. Check the scoreboard. Imagine 10,000 payments, 200 of them fraud.
184of 200 fraud cases caught
607of 9,800 good payments wrongly blocked
23%of blocked payments are real fraud
